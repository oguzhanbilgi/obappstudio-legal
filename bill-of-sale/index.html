What is a bill of sale?

A bill of sale is a short written record of a private sale. It names the seller and the buyer, describes what changed hands, and states the price and the date. When you sell a used car, a motorcycle, a boat or a trailer to another person instead of through a dealer, it gives both of you the same written account of what was agreed: who sold what to whom, for how much and on which terms.

For a titled vehicle or boat, the bill of sale usually sits next to the title rather than taking its place. Ownership is normally transferred by signing over the title and registering the change with your state. The bill of sale adds what a title often leaves out, such as the agreed price, how it was paid, whether part of it is still owed and whether the item was sold as is. Some states ask for a bill of sale at registration, some publish their own form, and some do not ask for one at all. Look up the rules on your state's DMV website, or the agency that registers boats, before the sale.

What to include in a bill of sale

  • The date of the sale and the state where it takes place.
  • The full names and addresses of the seller and the buyer. A phone number and a driver's license or ID number make each person easier to identify later.
  • A clear description of the item: year, make, model, body type and color, plus the VIN for a car, truck or motorcycle, the hull identification number (HIN) for a boat, or a serial number for a trailer, equipment or other goods.
  • The odometer reading for a motor vehicle.
  • The sale price, how it is paid, and any deposit and balance still due.
  • Whether the item is sold as is or with a warranty, and what that warranty covers.
  • Anything that comes with the sale, such as spare keys, accessories, a boat trailer or service records.
  • The signatures of both parties, the date and their printed names.

Copy the VIN or HIN exactly as it appears on the item and check it against the title. A single wrong character can cause trouble when the buyer registers the item.

Selling a car "as is"

Most private sales are as-is sales. The buyer takes the item in its current condition, with whatever faults it has, and the seller does not promise to fix anything after the sale. The as-is clause in this template says that the seller gives no warranty, express or implied, and that the buyer had the chance to inspect the item before buying it.

As a buyer, look the item over, take a test drive and consider an independent inspection before you sign. As a seller, describe the item honestly: an as-is clause covers ordinary wear and faults nobody knew about, but it may not protect a seller who hides a known problem or misstates the mileage. If you do offer a warranty, for example 30 days on the engine, choose the warranty option and write down exactly what is covered, for how long and what the buyer has to do to use it. Vague promises lead to disputes.

Odometer disclosure

When a motor vehicle changes hands, the seller is usually expected to state the mileage in writing, often on the title itself or on a separate form. The optional statement in this generator records the reading and whether, to the seller's knowledge, it is the actual mileage, exceeds the odometer's mechanical limits or is not the actual mileage. It is a written record between the two of you and does not replace a disclosure your state asks for.

Frequently asked questions

Is this bill of sale generator free?

Yes. There is no charge and no sign-up. You fill in the form, check the preview and print the document or save it as a PDF. The page runs in your browser: it does not upload or save what you type, sets no cookies and has no trackers.

Do I need a bill of sale to sell a car?

It depends on your state. Some states ask for a bill of sale when the buyer registers a vehicle bought from a private seller, others only recommend one, and several publish their own form that you are expected to use. Even where it is not required, a signed bill of sale gives both sides a written record of the price, the date and the terms. Check your state's DMV website before you meet the buyer.

Does a bill of sale replace the title?

No. For a titled vehicle, boat or trailer, ownership is normally transferred by signing the title over to the buyer and registering the change with the state. A bill of sale records the terms of the sale alongside the title. Keep both, and follow your state's steps for the title transfer.

Does a bill of sale have to be notarized?

It depends on your state and on what is being sold. Some states require a notarized bill of sale or a notarized title for certain sales, and many do not. This page does not notarize anything. If a notary is required where you live, print the document and sign it in front of one.

What does "sold as is" mean?

The buyer accepts the item in its current condition, with all known and unknown faults, and the seller gives no warranty after the sale. It does not excuse hiding a known defect or misstating the mileage. If you agree on any warranty, choose the warranty option and write the terms out in full.

Can I use it for a boat, a trailer or another item?

Yes. Choose the item type at the top of the form. For a boat, enter the hull identification number (HIN) and list a motor or trailer included in the sale in the notes. For a trailer, equipment or other goods, use the serial number and a short description. Boats and trailers may also need to be titled or registered, depending on your state.
